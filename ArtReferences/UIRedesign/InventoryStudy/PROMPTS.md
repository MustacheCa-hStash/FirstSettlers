# Inventory mockup prompts

Built-in ImageGen, October 10, 2026. Four separate transparent UI generations using the implemented stone-and-pillars building menu as a style reference. Screenshots were preserved by deterministic JavaScript compositing, not sent for background regeneration. compose.cjs cleans stray alpha outside the intended UI regions and verifies unchanged pixels. The gameplay hotbar layer is reduced to 60% of the modal-screen scale and anchored at the bottom for a compact in-play presentation.

## Shared prompt

Use case: ui-mockup. Generate an ISOLATED full game UI layer with GENUINELY TRANSPARENT ALPHA BACKGROUND for First Settlers, to composite over unchanged original gameplay screenshots.
Image 1 is a STYLE REFERENCE ONLY: the implemented building UI. Reuse its pale warm limestone/plaster panels, narrow classical pillar decorations along outer edges, tiny restrained incised square corner motifs, slate-blue selection fill, dark charcoal serif headings (similar to Georgia) and very legible plain humanist sans-serif body (similar to Segoe UI). Same mature restrained texture and simple matte stylized model thumbnails, no shiny gold, no goofy lettering, no huge ornaments, no rainbow rarity colors, no currencies or XP.
Important: generate only UI. Entire canvas outside interface is truly transparent alpha. No background scene, trees, sky, grass, water, rocks, vignette, global darkening or atmospheric blur. Interiors are clean stone material with no scenery baked into them. Transparent spaces between panels. Wide 16:9 canvas, same interface scale as reference. All UI resides within x4%-96%, y6%-95% of canvas. Pillars thin and subordinate, not a whole temple. No explanatory annotations, no title saying concept.
New inventory systems are design proposals. Item icons: simple readable matte low-poly logs, ordinary stones, bundled plant fiber, coiled rope, berries, meat, stone axe with timber handle, wooden hammer, torch, flint, hide and reeds. Realistic simple period tools, not fantasy magical gear or modern items.
Shared inventory example: 24 backpack cells in a 6-column by 4-row grid, with exactly 12 occupied cells: Wood 20, Stone 16, Fiber 6, Rope 4, Berries 8, Meat 2, Stone axe 1, Wooden hammer 1, Torch 1, Flint 7, Hide 3, Reeds 12. The other twelve cells empty. Counts occupy bottom-right in stack slots. Tools have a separate short durability track; count must not overlap track. Stone axe durability 68/100. Equipment and hotbar icons refer to the same carried items; they are not extra stacks.
Shared 9-slot quick-access order: 1 Stone axe, 2 Wooden hammer, 3 Torch, 4 Wood (20), 5 Stone (16), 6 Fiber (6), 7 Rope (4), 8 Berries (8), 9 Empty. Draw number keys small at top-left, stack counts bottom-right. Slot 1 selected slate-blue. Tools show separate durability tracks. Preserve ordering and counts across views. Labels are crisp readable English with sensible spacing. No sprawling item-stat paragraphs.
