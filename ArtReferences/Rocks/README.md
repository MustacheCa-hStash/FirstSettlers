# Painted rock surface

The October 12 style revision replaces dense procedural stone grooves with a generated painted albedo: broad muted grey/taupe/slate patches with minimal grain. `Assets/Textures/Rock/Presets/PaintedStone_Albedo.png` is the selected project asset. Source resolution is 1254 square; Unity imports it at 512 with mipmaps, repeat wrapping and four-times anisotropy.

Created using the built-in ImageGen tool, with the user's game screenshot as a style reference. The exact generation prompt is below. The generated image was copied into the project, and its original generated copy remains in the Codex image library.

The existing `WeatheredStone_ColorHeight.png` remains the coating-control map. Its bytes were preserved (SHA-256 `33d1b193979d78ad0a34bf8a2fb46af1ef78fd54b41b73dc3536495e257efc13`). The shader now reads snow and moss alpha from `_CoatingMap` independently of the new `_BaseMap` color. Snow uses the original noise, upward-normal thresholds, lower-height cutoff, tint, grain variation and coverage. Moss shifts its patch interval to 0.29–0.57 from 0.32–0.60, and coverage rises to 0.96 from 0.88. Across the mask pixels this raises mean upward-face patch opacity from 0.580 to 0.733, providing moderately fuller moss while retaining gaps. Surface relief is disabled to keep the finish soft and painted.

The forward shader still has three triplanar color lookups plus two coating/grain lookups, with an early exit for bare coating. No geometry, draw passes, generation, LOD, collider or pooling changes are part of this revision. There is one additional shared 512-pixel texture; disabling relief skips its derivative-normal calculation. The native camera benchmark records frame costs separately from live-game FPS.

The final dense-snow benchmark used seed 14566 as an isolated command-line override; the user's saved seed 14567 was preserved. Its optimized camera-render medians were 0.902 and 0.968 ms at 1280 × 720, compared with 0.976–1.019 ms in the previous surface profile. This is a rock-only editor camera test, not a full-game FPS measurement. The report is `.utmp/rocks/painted-seed14566-profile.txt`; use `Tools/ValidateRocks.ps1 -Benchmark -Seed 14566` to reproduce it.

Use **Tools > Terrain > Update Rock Surface Materials** to refresh the three shared materials without rebuilding meshes or prefabs. `Tools/ValidateRocks.ps1 -Surfaces` does this in the isolated project. The Blender source packs the painted albedo and uses smooth geometry without surface bump.

## Exact generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: a seamless tiling base-color texture for smooth stylized 3D rocks in a Unity game. The attached image is ONLY a style/context reference: its colorful simple grass, orange trees, and soft mountains are the environment the stone must fit. Do not reproduce the screenshot, landscape, rock object, UI, or lighting. Generate ONE square flat texture swatch, edge-to-edge and tileable on all four sides. Material: understated hand-painted stone, muted medium warm grey with very subtle cool slate and taupe patches. Broad softly blended angular mineral color planes, a few simplified short softened mineral strokes. Calm low contrast with a restrained painted finish, recognizable as stone while looking stylized, pleasant and simplified. Avoid photorealism, photographic grain, tiny noisy speckles, rough grooves, strong swirled veins, cracks forming grids, repeated cobblestones, dramatic outlines, facets drawn as polygons, highlights, directional shading, or ambient-occlusion shadows. No moss, snow, grass, plants, transparency, labels, border, text, or objects. This is albedo only: evenly lit, no baked lighting. Keep microdetail minimal and make the opposite edges match seamlessly. Aim for roughly 6 to 10 softly varying broad patches across the entire tile rather than dozens of tiny marks.
